How a tier is decided

One measurement underlies every rule: 30 days of Gate 1-hour bars, 36,029 observations across 39 crypto perps. Outcome measured: did price fall 10% within 12 hours. Base rate 6.0%. Symbols split odd/even into train and test, each half scored against its own base rate — the halves differed 1.6×, so a pooled denominator would make every lift wrong.

Tiers come from combinations measured jointly, never from multiplying separate lifts — OI change and 24h return are correlated, and treating them as independent would manufacture confidence that was never measured.

Open interest is the signal, in either direction. Still building or violently unwinding both precede dumps; quiet OI sits at 0.5× — half base.

Taker ratio is anti-predictive at the extremes, confirmed on Gate and Binance alike. Only the neutral 0.95–1.05 band is elevated, so it is deliberately not scored as bearish.

PRIME is ported from the older tamad-scanner, whose top band measured sharper (76% hit) than anything derived here, at the cost of firing on only ~0.5% of hours. Three of its components measured backwards on its own data — its taker term, its near-the-high bonus, and scoring open interest only when falling — and are corrected here. Fixed, it went 71.1% → 76.1% while firing more often.

The score ranks coins within a tier and is not a probability: strongest rule that fired, plus a small bonus per corroborating rule.

Scanner only — it ranks and records, it never trades.
